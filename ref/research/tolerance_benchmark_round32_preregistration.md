# Round 32 — pre-registration

Registered **before any further verification**, in a commit containing no results.

## Why this round exists

Round 31 asked whether corrections carry a higher defect rate than the text they correct, verified
**35** numeric claims across 33 `Fix #` commits, found **0** wrong at write, and declared the test
**indeterminate rather than negative**:

    P(0 wrong | p = 0.0228, n = 35) = 0.446
    n needed for P(0) < 0.05        = 130

Round 32 does the only thing that turns that into an answer: **keep verifying**. Nothing else about
the question changes.

## The trap this round has to avoid

The obvious failure mode is **stopping when something turns up**. Verify until the first wrong claim
appears and the rate is guaranteed to look bad; verify until bored and it looks good. Either way the
stopping rule, not the documents, produces the number. Round 23's Fisher test and round 31's power
calculation are both in this file's tradition, and both would be worthless under an outcome-dependent
stop.

**So the stopping rule is fixed here, in advance, and it is not a count:**

> **Verify every unverified claim-bearing line in round 31's committed inventory. Exhaust it. Then
> report whatever n that yields, and the power that n actually buys — including if it is short of
> 130.**

Exhaustion is outcome-independent by construction. A target of "130" is not: it would licence
stopping early on a good run or padding a thin one.

## Method, unchanged from round 31 on purpose

- **Population**: the same 33 `Fix #` commits, rounds 24–31 (PRs #114–#193).
- **Inventory**: the same 155 claim-bearing `.md` lines those commits added, regenerated by the same
  extractor from the same `commits.tsv`. **35 claims** drawn from it are already verified and are
  **carried forward, not re-verified** — re-verifying them could only inflate n without adding
  information.
- **Unit**: a numeric claim — a count, rate, n, or range endpoint asserted about the work. Not:
  tolerance values, resolutions, PDB ids, issue numbers, dates, version strings. Round 28's rule
  verbatim, for the third time, because changing it now would break comparability with both prior
  arms.
- **Verdict**: verifiable / unverifiable, and each verifiable one correct / wrong, by **re-deriving
  from the source** — not by pattern-matching, and not by whether an issue was ever filed.
- A line may carry **zero** claims under that rule (a section number, a code identifier). Those are
  recorded as such and are **not** padding for n.

## Predictions

**P1 — the correction arm's wrong-claim rate exceeds round 28's ~2.3 %.** Carried over verbatim from
round 31, where it could not be resolved. *Falsified* if the exhausted rate is at or below 2.3 %
**and** n is large enough for that to mean something, which the power calculation will state
explicitly rather than assume.

**P2 — the dominant cause among any wrong claims is SCOPE, not memory.** Carried over. Still the fork
on `round_figures.py`'s value: deriving a count does not tell you which population it should range
over.

**P3 — a correction-of-a-correction is more defective than a first correction.** Carried over.

**P5 — the exhausted inventory yields fewer than 130 verified claims.** New, and registered because it
is the likeliest outcome and would otherwise be reported as a disappointment rather than a result.
155 lines do not give 155 claims: round 31's own sample ran well under one claim per line after
excluding section numbers and code spans. *Falsified* if n ≥ 130. If P5 holds, **the honest conclusion
is that this population cannot power the test at all**, and the next round must widen the population
or abandon the question — not verify the same lines harder.

## What this round cannot answer

- **Whether the inventory itself is complete.** It is one extractor over one commit set. Claims in PR
  bodies and issue text remain outside it, and #204 — one of the seven defects that motivated round 31
  — lived in a PR body.
- **Whether "wrong at write" was judged consistently across 155 lines by one reader.** No second
  rater. Round 31's `#167c`/`#177c` pairing error is direct evidence that single-rater classification
  in this repo does drift.
- **Anything causal**, exactly as round 31 disclaimed.
- **Whether a fix would work.** Nothing is gated this round regardless of outcome. Rounds 29, 30 and
  31 each declined to build on a falsified or indeterminate premise; that precedent holds.
